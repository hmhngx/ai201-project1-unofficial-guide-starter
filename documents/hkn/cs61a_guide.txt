Overview
--------
CS 61A is not about learning a particular programming language but focuses on abstraction and problem-solving techniques. The course uses Python, Scheme, and SQL to teach core computer science concepts including functional programming, data abstraction, and object-oriented approaches. It is widely considered one of the most important foundational courses in the CS curriculum at UC Berkeley.

Prerequisites & Alternatives
------------------------------
Math 1A is required (may be taken concurrently). There are no formal programming prerequisites, though most students have some prior coding experience. The guide suggests CS 10 or Data 8/CS 8 as lighter alternatives for those seeking a gentler introduction to programming concepts before jumping into 61A.

Major Topics
------------
The curriculum spans the following areas:
- Functions and higher-order functions
- Data structures: lists, trees, linked lists
- Object-oriented programming
- Recursion and tree recursion
- Iterators and generators
- Interpreters and macros
- SQL and declarative programming
- Scheme (a dialect of Lisp)

Workload
--------
Weekly commitments include approximately 3 lecture hours, 1.5 lab hours, 1.5 discussion hours, plus 3-4 hours of independent study. Project weeks require significantly more time — typically 6-10 additional hours per major project.

Assignments include:
- Weekly coding assignments (effort-graded)
- Labs (completed in section or independently)
- Four major projects throughout the semester
- Two to three midterm exams
- One final exam

Tips and Advice
---------------
Staying current with lectures and assignments is essential. As the guide notes: "It is very easy to get behind in this course and since it can be fast-paced, it can be difficult to catch up." Students are strongly encouraged to gauge their readiness before enrollment by reviewing past course materials.

Understanding recursion before or during the first weeks of the course is critical. Students who struggle with recursion early on are more likely to fall behind.

Office hours and tutoring resources are heavily utilized by successful students. The course staff generally holds many office hours, especially before project deadlines.

Start projects early — do not wait until the last day. Projects are complex and often require multiple sessions of debugging and iteration.
